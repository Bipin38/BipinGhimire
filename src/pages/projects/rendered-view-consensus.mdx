## Context

Given a prebuilt 3D Gaussian Splatting scene and a free-form language query, find the object the query refers to — without training anything. Rendered-View Consensus (RVC) is a training-free method I co-developed; I’m the first-listed author on the manuscript.

### Key choices

- **Consensus across views, not one view.** Grounding evidence comes from 12 rendered views at complementary positions, combined as geometric support on the Gaussians, weighted by opacity and visibility.
- **Location and boundary are separate problems.** 3D consensus decides where the object is; SAM draws its boundary on the query view.
- **Verify before selecting.** Candidate masks pass geometric agreement checks and a semantic yes / no check before arbitration.

## Results

On LERF-OVS (four scenes, 208 records):

- **79.6 mIoU** for 2D query-view masks — **14.6 points** above the strongest published comparison in the same output regime.
- **75.8 mIoU** in an equal-model evaluation, to separate the method from the choice of backbone.

## What the numbers don’t say

- 2D masks and persistent 3D Gaussian outputs are different output contracts. For the frozen method the gap is 29.6 points (79.6 vs 50.0 mIoU), so the 2D number shouldn’t be read as 3D segmentation quality.
- Small or visually similar instances are harder; the analysis covers geometric failure modes and boundary quality.
- Output regime and potential evaluation-image leakage both affect zero-shot comparisons, and the paper examines both.

## Exploratory follow-up

- Batched multi-view / label processing ran about **5.2× faster** (≈0.20 s vs 1.08 s in a reported test configuration).
- The 3D-output score on LERF-OVS moved from 60.80 to 61.37 after a parsing fix, and to 62.18 with reliability weighting.
- Some view-selection gains didn’t reproduce zero-shot on 3D-OVS, so they aren’t treated as general improvements.
- <a href={withBase('#locus')}>LOCUS</a> extends the approach to small objects on a smart-glasses compute budget.
